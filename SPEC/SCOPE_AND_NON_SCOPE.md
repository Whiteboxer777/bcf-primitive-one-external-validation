# `SPEC/SCOPE_AND_NON_SCOPE.md`

## 0. Status

This document is the normative scope-and-non-scope specification for **BCF Primitive One**.
It defines exactly what belongs to the primitive, what does not, what kinds of authority the primitive does and does not claim, and what interpretive inflation or erosion is forbidden.

---

## 1. Canonical scope statement

The canonical scope of BCF Primitive One is:

> a deterministic, fail-closed, verifier-backed, closed-world admission boundary that determines whether a candidate request may lawfully pass a canonical verified bundle-bound boundary and yield a lawful permit for release of the bound action descriptor.

Everything inside scope must reduce to this. Everything outside scope must not be silently imported back into it.

---

## 2. Primary in-scope problem

The primary problem the primitive solves is:
> the lawful, deterministic, replay-attributable passage or non-passage of a candidate request through a canonical admission boundary before positive release authority is realized.

Inside scope are:
- primitive identity
- bundle validity and boundary-object fitness
- verification
- request conformance
- canonical identity
- structured permit/refusal
- replay-attributable semantics where required
- official positive-path discipline

---

## 3. Primary non-problems

BCF Primitive One does not itself solve:
- open-world truth
- general intelligence
- optimization
- broad legal compliance
- broad organizational governance
- moral philosophy
- external-world correctness in all senses
- infrastructure hardening in all senses
- universal safety guarantees
- generic workflow orchestration

These may relate to larger systems around the primitive, but they are not primitive meaning.

---

## 4. Authority scope

The primitive carries only **admission authority** of the following kind:
> authority to determine whether a candidate request may pass the law-governed bundle-bound boundary and, if so, realize lawful permit for release of the bound action descriptor.

Out of scope:
- truth authority
- scientific authority
- final legal authority across jurisdictions
- moral authority
- hardware enforcement authority
- unrestricted execution authority
- universal trust authority

---

## 5. World-model scope

The primitive is **closed-world**.
Only explicitly admitted structures, objects, bindings, and relations may carry positive authority.

Out of scope:
- open-world positive reasoning
- inferred completion of missing authority-bearing data
- permissive treatment of unknown authority-bearing structure
- hidden contextual assumptions as positive authority

---

## 6. Verification-boundedness

Verification inside scope means only:
> the candidate bundle is lawfully fit to serve as the admission boundary under the active primitive line.

It does not mean:
- universal trust
- universal external audit
- hardware-root attestation
- universal safety
- universal legality

---

## 7. Permit-boundedness

A permit means only:
> structured positive authority bound to the canonical admitted context and its lawful action-release meaning under the active primitive line.

It does not mean:
- unrestricted authorization
- downstream completion
- globally legal or morally ideal behavior
- generic endorsement of actor or system

---

## 8. Infrastructural scope

In scope:
- logical and semantic non-bypass at primitive-law level
- one official positive path
- deterministic boundary semantics
- structured outputs
- replay-attributable semantics where required
- fail-closed boundary behavior

Out of scope unless separately established elsewhere:
- kernel-level enforcement
- hypervisor-level isolation
- hardware-rooted attestation
- TPM/TEE guarantees
- HSM-backed guarantees
- physical tamper resistance
- OS-level non-bypass guarantees
- deployment hardening guarantees

---

## 9. Legal, safety, truth, and ML scope

### Legal/regulatory
The primitive may support regulated workflows, but it is not itself compliance.

### Safety
The primitive contributes fail-closed boundary discipline. It is not universal downstream safety.

### Truth/correctness
The primitive establishes correctness only relative to its own law line, not universal open-world truth.

### Machine learning
The primitive may constrain ML-driven action release. It is not itself an ML model or universal AI safety stack.

---

## 10. Anti-inflation rules

No lawful interpretation may inflate the primitive into:
- truth engine
- universal safety engine
- universal legality engine
- total governance engine
- hardware/OS enforcement guarantee
- unrestricted trust engine

---

## 11. Anti-erosion rules

No lawful interpretation may flatten the primitive into:
- mere logging
- mere file checking
- mere booleans
- generic workflow glue
- informal policy prose

---

## 12. Boundary between primitive and surrounding system

A larger system may contain the primitive, but is not identical to the primitive.
The primitive does not automatically inherit every property of the surrounding system, and the surrounding system does not automatically inherit primitive conformance merely by containing it.

---

## 13. Theorem commitments

The line commits at minimum to:
- admission-boundary theorem
- non-truth theorem
- non-universal-safety theorem
- verification-boundedness theorem
- permit-boundedness theorem
- closed-world theorem of scope
- non-infrastructure theorem
- surrounding-system distinction theorem
- anti-inflation theorem
- anti-erosion theorem

---

## 14. Final scope-and-non-scope law statement

BCF Primitive One is, and only is, a deterministic, fail-closed, verifier-backed, closed-world admission boundary whose lawful task is to determine whether a candidate request may pass a canonical verified bundle-bound contract and thereby yield structured positive permit for release of the bound action descriptor, while otherwise yielding structured refusal; accordingly, everything required for primitive identity, bundle validity, verification, canonical identity, request conformance, permit/refusal realization, replay-attributable semantics, and official positive-path discipline lies inside scope, and everything purporting to add open-world truth judgment, universal safety, universal legality, total governance, hardware or OS enforcement, unrestricted trust, generic workflow orchestration, or any other broader authority not explicitly grounded in the law line lies outside scope unless separately and explicitly established beyond the primitive.

That is the scope and non-scope boundary.
